Queensland Public Dental Clinics

How effectively are Queensland public dental catchments
managing general type patient demand?

Executive Summary

Treatment rates are insufficient across most quarters. Larger catchments appear stable despite having treatment rates that predict backlog accumulation. Smaller catchments are presenting pressurised failure with consistent severe backlogs.
The broader system is partially relieved by patient attrition rather than treatment. In contrast, Torres and Cape, a remote marginalised catchment with limited public dental clinics, reveals a patient base with no viable exit from the waitlist.

Introducing the Queensland Public Dental Waitlist.

Oral health services data is collected from Queensland Health dental clinics and fetched from Queensland Government Open Data Portal.

To describe this dataset:
Each row represents a single clinic (identified by unique id and clinic name) within a single reporting period, containing fields for the clinic's catchment, appointment service, and waiting and treated patients segmented by wait time tier. Reporting period frequency has changed from monthly in 2023 to quarterly going forward. Overall the dataset captures the status of waiting and treated patients across clinics in Queensland over time.

Data Transformation Decisions

Normalisation:
Why pivot and expand the wide file format?
The source file comes in a wide flat format where each row includes a column per wait time tier. This structure does not support aggregation of the patient base. These tier columns are unpivoted into individual rows.

Time-Series:
Why analyse the data from July 2023?
This dataset is split by two collection methods; records are monthly and in 2023 become quarterly. The dataset's first year (2020) shows missing months, and later years missing entire quarters. Monthly records were reviewed at a catchment level to assure completeness before aggregating monthly data into quarterly periods. But there is marginal benefit in transforming the available April–June 2023 monthly records into a comparable quarter, which does not outweigh the simplicity of working entirely within a quarterly collection period.
Analysis begins Q3 2023, alongside commencement of quarterly collection.

Aggregation:
How are patient figures kept consistent across collection method changes?
Waiting patients are a snapshot in time (showing how many are sitting in the queue at the end of a period). For the monthly collection period, the final month-end of each complete quarter is used. Treated patients are a flow (how many passed through) and are summed across months. The extent of partial or missing months and quarters are highlighted in the visualisation below, adding perspective to the scope of consistent data.

Analytical Framework

What is effective? The system is effective when patients waiting are treated within desired wait periods.

Segmentation:

Why focus only on General Appointments?
Their large scale can influence overall system performance, making them a plausible anchor for this analysis. While other appointment types are out of focus here, they may surface useful findings in future analyses.

What is a General Appointment?
It is described in the source as 'General Care' or a routine check up or dental treatment. Uncertain on the scope of dental care for this type of appointment - whether minor and or major treatment.

Is treatment keeping pace with demand?


To better understand this question, we will use the treatment rate to determine a waitlist's expected completion time in months, then compare that figure to the desired wait time for a General appointment.

What is the treatment rate?
It is the ratio of treated to waiting patients per quarter. General appointments have a desired wait time of 24months. Using a treatment rate we can gauge the capacity of a waitlist against that benchmark. When a quarter requires more than 24 months to clear its waitlist volume, it is operating over capacity.

Quarters have treatment rates expected to go beyond desired timeframes,
what happens to the remaining patients?


Who are the remaining patients?

A 'Silhouette of Participation' emerges from the patient base as a result of these treatment rates. To frame this silhouette we exclude two patient groups: The expected throughput of patients after 24 months at treatment rate, and the patients who are experiencing undesirable wait times (25 to 60+ months). At a catchment level, these remaining figures inform the depth of pressure experienced across waitlists.

By using a scale of participation, we can see which catchments have patient demand quietly exiting while in-turn pulling the treatment rate lower and which catchments have more patients remaining in excess wait times. The silhouette (shades of violet) represents the gap of unaccounted patients. On the other end of the scale (coral), catchments are experiencing higher retention in backlog. The negative space (white) represents quarters where a catchment has the desired treatment rate of 24 months.

Reasoning & Limitations:
Absolute figures are not normalised by catchment size. Numbers are right for the silhouette because it is a concrete point of magnitude - a percentage would get lost without reference. Overlaying percentage of catchment size would also be confusing to decode.
The shape of patients with undesirable wait times are not showcased using this metric and their extent is examined separately. Specific time bands of wait will be observed next in "Backlog Pressure".

Backlog Pressure

Which catchments are experiencing the most pressure to backlog?


What is backlog?

The waitlist has segmented patients into time bands (tiers), allowing for patients to be weighted by tier. Backlog pressure weights delayed patients relative to how far their wait exceeds the desired threshold. Patients within the desired threshold are assigned a weight of 0 and excluded from backlog pressure. For patients waiting beyond 24 months(the desired wait), 24 months is deducted from the average of their time band. Remaining months are then divided by 24 months. 1 is added to ensure patients carry a weight above 1, so multiplication amplifies rather than reduces their count.

Weighting Formula:
( x ¯ − 24 24 + 1 ) × patient = weighted patient
How is pressure measured?
Pressure is expressed as the proportion of backlog to the weighted waitlist overall, where zero indicates no backlog pressure.


Why is backlog pressure important to insights on effectiveness and the silhouette of participation within catchments?

The effectiveness of a catchment's waitlist process is tied to the presence of long-waiting patients. This measure divides catchments with intensive backlog from those free of backlog.The silhouette of participation shows the reaction to treatment rates without bringing to light the extent of wait pressure within catchments. Backlog Pressure disproves the possibility of an equivalent body of long-waiting patients obscuring patient exits and it confirms that the unaccounted patients in the silhoutte are genuine exits.

Reasoning & Limitations: It is suitable to measure proportional percentage in backlog pressure because the metric is a ratio by design with no meaningful absolute.

Discussion

The purpose of this analysis is to gain insight on catchment demand, and effectiveness demonstrated by the Queensland public dental system at a catchment level for general appointments.

Treatment rates break sustainable thresholds regularly quarter on quarter ("Is treatment keeping pace with demand?"). This points to patients on average experiencing longer wait times in line with a waitlist's overflow. Analysing the remaining overflow led to distinguishing two patient types. Patients belonging to large catchments were leaving in large numbers before 24 months. In contrast, Torres and Cape presents timely treatment rates, while patients are stuck with an average wait of 3.5 years.

What do these findings mean for the future? Smaller more remote catchments will have clientele for longer, the interpretation is that these patients have limited alternatives. Waiting lists in catchments with a high amount of unexplained waiters will continue to leave before their appointment date materialises. Unexplained leavers include genuine dropout seeking private care but also deaths or interstate moves.

The limitations that remain unknown are the possible reality of patients making multiple appointments with varying clinics within a catchment - this would distort waitlist expectations. The scope and quality of treatment provided at a general appointment level, considering the condition of clientele being treated after absorbitant periods. The extent of these findings across a wider time period (more than 2 years).

What comes next? A better understanding why patients are leaving and why they are staying is recommended. The administrative impact of patient exodus is unclear and warrants further investigation by business intelligence analysts.
